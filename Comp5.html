Competency Five

Assessing Student Learning

Description of Competency

Assessing student learning is critical for understanding my impact as a teacher. I need to know how much students understand in order to determine if my teaching is effective and what should change. The way I assess student learning may differ depending on the purpose of the course. For a lower level course, meant as a general overview of concepts and not meant to prepare students for a career in STEM, my assessments will be more focused on a general understanding of concepts and students application of skills to their own lives. For a higher level course, meant to prepare students for upper division courses and a career in a physics or astronomy fields, the assessments will be more geared toward illustrating how students use and apply their knowledge and less focused on memorization. The goal of my classes is not simply for students to pass but for them to gain a level of understanding with the course material where they can apply these concepts and skills to other courses or their own lives. To accomplish this, I will use a combination of formal and informal assessments.

    Reflection

    For students to be able to perform at the level you want, they need to know your learning goals. Making these as clearly communicated as possible is the first step to getting students where you want them to be. Each lesson will have a set of learning goals that students can view so they have an idea of what will be covered in class and what they will be expected to do. Additionally, the syllabus will have the higher level course goals and each test will have a list of tasks students should be able to do.

    Pre class assignments are structured as a short online reading or a video to watch prior to class. Students then are required to post one question they had about the reading or video. The purpose of this assignment is to get students thinking about the material prior to class and gives me a chance to see what are areas of confusion for the students. In class assessments will include I-clicker questions, used as concept checks, so I can see if students are following the lecture portion of the class. Problems are worked through in groups or pairs, allowing students to learn from each other. This in class work is graded for accuracy, completetion, and understanding. Students are instructed to explain their reasoning. At the end of class students will either complete a minute paper, concept map or reflection questions, these are only graded for completion and are used by me to see gaps in student understanding. Homework problems are there for students to practice these concepts and are a small percent of students' grades. They are also encouraged to work together on homeworks so long as they list the names of all the people they worked with. Students will have tests to assess their understanding of the concepts learned with some questions focused on the application of these concepts to real world problems.

    In the higher level courses students final assessment will be in the form of a final project. They will be expected to work together in a group to apply the concepts learned to a real world problem. This final project will be an opportunity to use multiple concepts together to solve a problem and to practice teamwork, project management and communication.
