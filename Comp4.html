Competency Four

Understanding University Context

Description of Competency

To create the right teaching environment for students it is vital that I understand the context in which I am teaching. Different educational institutions have different goals and values and it is critical that I understand what is being asked of me in order to perform my best. For example, at a Research University my role as a professor would likely include leading a research group of post docs, graduate students and undergraduate students. This leadership role would include mentorship, guiding students through their academic paths, writing and co-writing publications, teaching classes and writing grants to fund my research group.

Teaching at a community college would be vastly different, including more teaching and outreach. I must also consider how the type of class I teach will shape my instruction. Teaching large intro level courses will mean I have students who are not STEM majors and will have to alter activities to fit a larger number of students. For higher level courses I can expect that my students are more familiar with math and have prior physics experience. I can also employ more group work with a smaller class size. To effectively serve the population of students that come to my class I also need to keep in mind where the institution is located and who my students are. What are the economic backgrounds of my students and how might that show up in the class? What is the cultural context and how can I show up for my students to create inclusive and welcoming teaching environments?

    Reflection

    My academic journey started at Citrus Community College then continued at a state college at California State University Fullerton (CSUF) and finally to a large land grant institute, Michigan State University. My student experience at each of these institutions was unique. At Citrus my professors taught many classes and there were several programs to help prepare me for careers or higher education. I was supported by many faculty here to pursue a degree in physics. This environment was also the most diverse, I tutored and learned alongside people of all walks of life, veterans, high school students, parents, people coming back to school and people my age working toward transferring to a four year institution. At CSUF I worked in a research group for the first time. My professors taught smaller more specific courses and managed a group of student researchers. I also worked alongside post docs and was encouraged to present my work at conferences, go to networking events and guided in the graduate school application process. At MSU, I TA’d an introduction to astronomy lab for non-STEM majors. The class was about 80 students, the largest I’ve ever worked with. This required more time to prepare for lectures and lab work, grading homework and conducting office hours where I helped students more one on one. All of this experience has helped me to understand the different roles that teachers play at different kinds of institutions.
